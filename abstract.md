# Beyond Proportional Allocation: Transistor-Level Energy Attribution for Containerized ML Workloads

## Abstract

Accurate per-container energy attribution is a prerequisite for carbon-aware billing, workload placement, and sustainability compliance in cloud environments. Existing container energy monitoring tools, including cAdvisor, Kepler, and Scaphandre, distribute host energy across containers proportionally to CPU or GPU utilization time. This strategy assumes that all workloads consume energy at the same rate per unit of utilization and that operating conditions remain stable across time. Neither assumption holds for machine learning inference, where memory-bound and compute-bound workloads differ in energy intensity by an order of magnitude, and sustained GPU utilization raises die temperature enough to trigger clock throttling and shift the power curve. We present a physics-grounded alternative based on the Transistor Operations for Machine Learning (TOML) framework, which traces per-operation energy costs to CMOS switching dynamics. We extend TOML with three new metrics designed for multi-tenant container environments: Thermal Efficiency Decay (TED), which quantifies how energy per transistor operation degrades as die temperature rises under sustained load; Sustained Power Proportionality (SPP), which measures the linearity of power draw relative to useful transistor-operation throughput; and Co-location Thermal Interference (CTI), which captures the energy overhead that one container imposes on its neighbors through shared-die thermal coupling. We containerize seven ML architectures spanning CNNs, RNNs, LSTMs, GRUs, and gradient-boosted trees, then compare energy attribution under proportional allocation against TOML-derived estimates in both isolated and co-located deployment scenarios. Proportional allocation misattributes container energy by up to 4.7x for memory-dominated workloads running alongside compute-intensive ones, and thermal throttling introduces an additional 15 to 30 percent attribution error that utilization-based methods cannot detect. We propose a container-aware energy attribution layer that integrates TOML metrics into standard cloud observability stacks using only GPU performance counters already available through existing driver interfaces.
